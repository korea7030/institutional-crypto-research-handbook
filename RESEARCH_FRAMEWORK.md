Institutional Research Framework (IRF)

How Professional Researchers Think

Version 1.0

⸻

Purpose

This document describes how Institutional Research Methodology (IRM) is applied in practice.

While RESEARCH_STANDARD.md defines research rules, this document defines research behavior.

It explains how professional researchers transform observations into evidence-based conclusions.

⸻

Research Is Not Prediction

The primary objective of research is not to predict future prices.

The objective is to continuously improve decision quality through structured evidence collection and evaluation.

Good research reduces uncertainty.

It never eliminates uncertainty.

⸻

⸻

Step 1 — Observation

Observation begins with measurable facts.

Examples

* BTC Exchange Reserve decreased.
* ETH Gas Fees increased.
* Stablecoin supply expanded.
* Active addresses reached yearly highs.

Rules

Observations must contain no interpretation.

⸻

Step 2 — Question

Every observation generates questions.

Examples

Why?

Why now?

Why this protocol?

Who caused it?

Has this happened before?

Questions define research quality.

Poor questions produce poor research.

⸻

Step 3 — Evidence Collection

Collect evidence before explaining observations.

Evidence categories

* Onchain Data
* Official Documentation
* Protocol Specifications
* Academic Literature
* Market Structure
* Macro Environment

Multiple independent sources are preferred.

⸻

Step 4 — Evidence Validation

Every dataset must be validated.

Validation criteria

* Authenticity
* Freshness
* Completeness
* Independence
* Consistency

Unvalidated evidence should never support conclusions.

⸻

Step 5 — Counter Evidence

Professional researchers actively search for information that disproves their preferred explanation.

Question

What evidence would make my conclusion wrong?

Counter evidence strengthens research quality.

⸻

Step 6 — Alternative Hypotheses

Never stop after the first explanation.

Example

Observation

Exchange Reserve decreased.

Possible explanations

* Long-term accumulation
* ETF settlement
* Custody migration
* Bridge activity
* Internal restructuring

Professional researchers compare hypotheses.

They do not defend them.

⸻

Step 7 — Probability Assessment

Institutional research estimates probability.

Never certainty.

Example

Hypothesis A

65%

Hypothesis B

25%

Hypothesis C

10%

These values represent confidence based on evidence rather than prediction.

⸻

Step 8 — Conclusion

Conclusions summarize:

* Observation
* Supporting Evidence
* Counter Evidence
* Remaining Unknowns
* Confidence Level

Every conclusion should remain revisable.

⸻

Step 9 — Monitoring

Research continues after publication.

Researchers monitor

* New protocol upgrades
* New datasets
* Market reactions
* Macro developments
* Regulatory changes

Evidence may improve or weaken previous conclusions.

⸻

Step 10 — Revision

When better evidence appears:

Revise the research.

Do not defend outdated conclusions.

Professional integrity requires adaptation.

⸻

Institutional Thinking

Retail asks

“Is this bullish?”

Institution asks

“What evidence supports this interpretation?”

“What evidence contradicts it?”

“How reliable is the evidence?”

“What assumptions am I making?”

Institutional Mindset

Researchers should avoid asking

“What do I believe?”

Instead ask

“What does the evidence currently support?”

⸻

Common Failure Patterns

Professional researchers avoid

* Confirmation Bias
* Cherry Picking
* Narrative Investing
* Emotional Reasoning
* Overconfidence
* Ignoring Counter Evidence

⸻

Research Output Standard

Every completed research document should answer

What happened?

Why might it have happened?

Which evidence supports this?

Which evidence contradicts this?

Which assumptions remain?

How confident are we?

What should we monitor next?

⸻

Final Principle

Research is not the process of proving yourself correct.

Research is the process of continuously improving your understanding while remaining willing to be proven wrong.
