# Capstone Challenge – Blockchain Foundations

> **Research Unit:** BLOCKCHAIN-FOUNDATION-012

---

# Purpose

This Research Unit concludes the **Blockchain Foundations** module.

Rather than introducing new concepts,

this document evaluates whether the learner can integrate everything learned throughout the previous Research Units.

The objective is not memorization.

The objective is structured reasoning.

---

# Learning Objectives

After completing this Capstone Challenge, readers should be able to:

- Explain why blockchain was invented.
- Describe the relationship between blockchain and Bitcoin.
- Analyze blockchain using institutional thinking.
- Identify technical trade-offs.
- Distinguish facts from assumptions.
- Conduct basic blockchain research independently.

---

# Capstone Scenario

Imagine the following announcement.

---

**A startup introduces a new blockchain platform.**

The company claims:

- 1,000,000 Transactions Per Second
- Zero transaction fees
- Fully decentralized
- Maximum security
- Complete privacy
- Instant finality
- Better than Bitcoin
- Better than Ethereum

The company provides no technical paper.

Only marketing materials are available.

---

As an institutional researcher,

your task is **not** to decide whether the project is good or bad.

Your task is to investigate the claims.

---

# Exercise 1 — Observation

Separate observations from marketing.

Identify:

- Observable facts
- Marketing claims
- Unsupported statements

Do not draw conclusions.

---

# Exercise 2 — Questions

Write at least ten research questions.

Examples:

- Which consensus mechanism is used?
- How many validators exist?
- Is participation permissionless?
- How is decentralization measured?
- How is transaction throughput calculated?

Do not search for answers yet.

---

# Exercise 3 — Required Evidence

List the evidence required before accepting each claim.

Examples:

For:

```
1,000,000 TPS
```

Possible evidence:

- Independent benchmark
- Hardware specifications
- Network configuration
- Validator count
- Test methodology

Repeat this process for every major claim.

---

# Exercise 4 — Hidden Assumptions

Every system makes assumptions.

Identify assumptions behind statements such as:

- Fully decentralized
- Maximum security
- Instant finality

Ask:

"What assumptions must be true for this claim to hold?"

---

# Exercise 5 — Trade-off Analysis

Complete the following table.

| Claimed Property | Likely Trade-off |
|------------------|------------------|
| Higher TPS | ? |
| Zero Fees | ? |
| Instant Finality | ? |
| Complete Privacy | ? |
| Maximum Security | ? |

Remember:

Engineering requires trade-offs.

If no trade-off is presented,

additional investigation is required.

---

# Exercise 6 — Counter Evidence

Professional researchers actively search for contradictory evidence.

For each claim,

write:

"What evidence would make this claim false?"

Examples include:

- Validator concentration
- Permissioned architecture
- Benchmark inconsistencies
- Security assumptions

Counter evidence improves research quality.

---

# Exercise 7 — Institutional Thinking

Answer the following.

1. What problem does this blockchain attempt to solve?

2. Does the problem require blockchain?

3. Why not use PostgreSQL?

4. What advantage does decentralization provide?

5. Which trust assumptions remain?

Support every answer with reasoning.

---

# Exercise 8 — Risk Assessment

Identify technical risks.

Examples:

- Consensus failure
- Validator centralization
- Smart contract vulnerabilities
- Governance concentration
- Economic attacks

Rank risks by probability and impact.

---

# Exercise 9 — Final Assessment

Prepare a one-page institutional research summary.

Your report should contain:

## Observation

Only measurable facts.

---

## Evidence

Primary sources collected.

---

## Unknowns

Missing information.

---

## Assumptions

Explicit assumptions.

---

## Risks

Technical and operational risks.

---

## Confidence

Assign an Evidence Confidence Level (ECL).

---

## Conclusion

State your current conclusion.

Avoid certainty.

Document remaining uncertainty.

---

# Self-Assessment Checklist

Before finishing,

verify:

□ I separated facts from interpretations.

□ I searched for counter evidence.

□ I documented assumptions.

□ I identified unknowns.

□ I avoided marketing language.

□ I evaluated trade-offs.

□ I used primary sources whenever possible.

□ My conclusion matches the available evidence.
