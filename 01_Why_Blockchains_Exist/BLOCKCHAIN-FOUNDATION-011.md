# Research Questions

> **Research Unit:** BLOCKCHAIN-FOUNDATION-011

---

# Learning Objectives

After completing this Research Unit, readers should be able to:

- Build research questions from first principles.
- Distinguish good questions from poor questions.
- Separate observable facts from assumptions.
- Design a blockchain research process before searching for answers.
- Think like an institutional researcher rather than a content consumer.

---

# Key Questions

This Research Unit answers the following questions.

1. Why are research questions important?
2. What makes a research question useful?
3. How do institutions generate research questions?
4. How should blockchain research begin?
5. Which questions lead to meaningful insight?

---

# Introduction

Every research project begins with a question.

Poor questions produce poor research.

Strong questions produce useful knowledge.

Professional researchers spend significant time refining the question before collecting evidence.

The quality of the conclusion rarely exceeds the quality of the original question.

---

# Research Begins With Observation

Institutional research follows a simple principle.

```
Observation

↓

Question

↓

Evidence

↓

Analysis

↓

Conclusion
```

Notice that conclusions never come first.

Observation drives the entire process.

---

# Example

Observation

```
Bitcoin exchange reserves decreased.
```

Poor Question

```
Is Bitcoin going to increase?
```

Better Question

```
Which factors caused exchange reserves to decrease?
```

Professional Question

```
What proportion of the observed decrease can be explained by long-term accumulation, ETF custody movements, exchange wallet restructuring, or other measurable factors?
```

Each question increases research quality.

---

# Characteristics of Good Research Questions

Good research questions are:

- Observable
- Measurable
- Testable
- Falsifiable
- Evidence-driven

Poor research questions depend upon opinions.

---

# Characteristics of Poor Questions

Poor questions often begin with:

- Will...
- Should...
- Is this good?
- Which coin will explode?
- What should I buy?

These questions encourage speculation rather than investigation.

---

# First-Principles Questions

Professional blockchain researchers frequently begin with questions such as:

- What problem is this protocol solving?
- Why was this design chosen?
- Which assumptions does this system require?
- What happens if one assumption fails?
- Which trade-offs are being made?

These questions remain useful regardless of market conditions.

---

# Layered Questioning

Institutional research moves from simple to complex.

Level 1

```
What happened?
```

↓

Level 2

```
Why did it happen?
```

↓

Level 3

```
How do we know?
```

↓

Level 4

```
Could another explanation exist?
```

↓

Level 5

```
What evidence would invalidate this conclusion?
```

Each level reduces uncertainty.

# Categories of Blockchain Research Questions

## Technical

Examples:

- Why does Bitcoin use Proof of Work?
- How does Ethereum execute smart contracts?
- Why is block ordering important?

---

## Economic

Examples:

- How is token supply created?
- Which incentives encourage honest participation?
- What causes validator behavior?

---

## Network

Examples:

- How decentralized is the validator set?
- How are blocks propagated?
- What happens during a network partition?

---

## On-Chain

Examples:

- Which wallets accumulated?
- Which addresses distributed?
- Which entities generated transaction volume?

---

## Governance

Examples:

- Who controls protocol upgrades?
- How are consensus rules changed?
- Which participants influence governance?

---

# Asking Better Investment Questions

Instead of asking:

```
Should I buy Bitcoin?
```

Ask:

- What assumptions support Bitcoin's long-term thesis?
- Which indicators strengthen that thesis?
- Which indicators weaken it?
- Which future events would invalidate it?

These questions produce actionable research.

---

# Institutional Thinking

Professional researchers rarely ask:

```
What do I think?
```

Instead they ask:

```
What does the evidence currently support?
```

The difference appears subtle,

but it fundamentally changes the research process.

# Research Template

Every new blockchain investigation should begin with the following template.

```
Observation

↓

Research Question

↓

Available Evidence

↓

Missing Evidence

↓

Possible Hypotheses

↓

Counter Evidence

↓

Confidence Assessment

↓

Conclusion

↓

Monitoring Plan
```

---

# Counter Evidence

Even excellent questions have limitations.

Questions may become outdated because:

- Protocols evolve.
- Regulations change.
- Market structures shift.
- Better datasets become available.

Researchers should periodically revisit and refine earlier questions.

---

# Summary

Institutional research begins with disciplined questioning.

The objective is not to predict markets.

The objective is to reduce uncertainty through progressively better questions.

Professional researchers understand that strong conclusions are built upon strong questions,

and strong questions are built upon careful observation.
