The Institutional Crypto Research Handbook

Institutional Research Methodology (IRM) v1.0

Version: v1.0 (Foundation)

⸻

Purpose

This document defines the research methodology used throughout The Institutional Crypto Research Handbook.

It establishes how evidence is collected, evaluated, interpreted, challenged, and revised.

The objective is not to maximize confidence.

The objective is to maximize reliability.

Every chapter in this repository must follow this methodology.

⸻

Research Philosophy

Professional research is not the search for certainty.

It is the continuous reduction of uncertainty through evidence.

A conclusion is never considered “true.”

It is considered the best-supported explanation given currently available evidence.

Research therefore remains iterative.

Every conclusion may change when better evidence becomes available.

⸻

Institutional Research Principles

Principle 1 — Evidence Before Opinion

Every conclusion must originate from evidence.

Opinions without evidence are excluded.

Interpretations without evidence must be explicitly labeled.

⸻

Principle 2 — Primary Sources First

Whenever possible, primary sources must be consulted before any secondary interpretation.

Primary sources include:

* Protocol specifications
* Official documentation
* Whitepapers
* Standards (BIPs / EIPs)
* Academic publications
* Original datasets

Secondary sources should supplement, never replace, primary evidence.

⸻

Principle 3 — Traceability

Every important conclusion must be traceable back to its supporting evidence.

Readers should always be able to answer:

* Where did this information originate?
* Who published it?
* Can it be independently verified?

If traceability cannot be established, confidence must be reduced.

⸻

Principle 4 — Reproducibility

Research should be reproducible.

Whenever possible, another researcher should be able to reproduce the same conclusion using the cited sources and methodology.

⸻

Principle 5 — Intellectual Honesty

When evidence is incomplete:

State:

Unknown

When competing explanations exist:

Present each explanation fairly.

Never manufacture certainty.

Institutional Research Workflow (IRM)

Every analytical chapter follows the same workflow.

Observation

↓

Question

↓

Evidence Collection

↓

Evidence Validation

↓

Counter Evidence

↓

Alternative Hypotheses

↓

Probability Assessment

↓

Conclusion

↓

Monitoring

↓

Revision

This workflow is mandatory.

Skipping any stage requires explicit justification.

⸻

Observation Standard

Observations describe measurable reality.

Examples:

* Exchange reserve decreased.
* Active addresses increased.
* Funding rate turned positive.

Observations must not contain interpretation.

⸻

Question Standard

Every observation generates one or more research questions.

Examples:

* Why did exchange reserves decrease?
* Is this behavior historically unusual?
* Which alternative explanations exist?

The quality of research depends on the quality of questions.

⸻

Evidence Collection Standard

Evidence should be gathered from multiple independent sources whenever possible.

Preferred categories include:

* Protocol specifications
* Blockchain data
* Official documentation
* Academic research
* Independent datasets
* Institutional research

Evidence should never rely upon a single unverified source.

⸻

Evidence Validation

Collected evidence must be evaluated before interpretation.

Validation includes:

* Authenticity
* Source credibility
* Data freshness
* Internal consistency
* External consistency

Invalid evidence must be discarded.

⸻

Evidence Hierarchy

Priority order:

Tier 1

Official Specifications

Tier 2

Protocol Documentation

Tier 3

Academic Literature

Tier 4

Protocol Source Code

Tier 5

Institutional Research

Tier 6

Community Research

Lower-tier evidence may support—but not replace—higher-tier evidence.

⸻

Evidence Confidence Level (ECL)

Every significant claim receives an Evidence Confidence Level.

ECL-A

Directly supported by primary specifications.

Highest confidence.

⸻

ECL-B

Supported by multiple independent, high-quality sources.

Strong confidence.

⸻

ECL-C

Widely accepted professional interpretation.

Requires acknowledgment of assumptions.

⸻

ECL-D

Emerging hypothesis.

Requires further validation.

Not suitable for strong conclusions.

⸻

ECL-U

Unknown.

Current evidence insufficient.

Further investigation required.

⸻

Interpretation Standard

Interpretation begins only after evidence has been evaluated.

Interpretations must:

* Identify assumptions.
* Explain reasoning.
* Acknowledge limitations.
* Consider alternative explanations.

Interpretations should never be presented as facts.

⸻

Counter Evidence Standard

Every important conclusion must explicitly ask:

“What evidence would invalidate this conclusion?”

Counter evidence strengthens research by reducing confirmation bias.

Absence of counter evidence does not strengthen a conclusion.

⸻

Alternative Hypothesis Standard

Researchers should actively search for competing explanations.

Example:

Observation:

Exchange reserves declined.

Possible hypotheses:

* Long-term accumulation
* Custody migration
* ETF settlement
* Bridge activity
* Internal wallet restructuring

Research should compare competing hypotheses rather than selecting the first plausible explanation.

⸻

Probability Assessment

Institutional research estimates probability rather than certainty.

Confidence should reflect:

* Quantity of evidence
* Quality of evidence
* Consistency of evidence
* Historical reliability

Confidence must never exceed evidence quality.

⸻

Research Integrity

Researchers must:

* Distinguish facts from interpretation.
* Admit uncertainty.
* Document conflicting evidence.
* Update conclusions when better evidence appears.

Integrity is valued above confidence.

⸻

Common Cognitive Biases

Researchers should continuously monitor for:

* Confirmation Bias
* Anchoring Bias
* Availability Bias
* Narrative Bias
* Recency Bias
* Survivorship Bias
* Selection Bias

Bias awareness is part of every research process.

⸻

Research Checklist

Before publishing any chapter, verify:

□ Primary sources reviewed

□ Evidence independently verified

□ Alternative explanations considered

□ Counter evidence documented

□ Historical context included

□ Assumptions identified

□ Evidence Confidence Level assigned

□ Sources cited

□ Institutional Thinking section completed

□ Challenge questions included

⸻

Evidence Traceability

Every major conclusion should support backward traceability.

Example:

Conclusion

↓

Evidence

↓

Dataset

↓

Primary Source

↓

Original Protocol

Readers should be able to reconstruct the reasoning process.

⸻

Revision Policy

Research is never final.

Documents should be updated when:

* Protocol specifications change
* New academic evidence appears
* Better datasets become available
* Previous interpretations become obsolete

Version history must document significant conceptual changes.

⸻

Definition of Research Quality

A document is considered institutional-grade only if:

* Evidence is traceable.
* Reasoning is reproducible.
* Assumptions are explicit.
* Counterarguments are documented.
* Confidence matches evidence.
* Conclusions remain open to revision.

⸻

Guiding Principle

The objective of institutional research is not to be right.

The objective is to remain intellectually honest while continuously improving the probability of being correct through better evidence.
