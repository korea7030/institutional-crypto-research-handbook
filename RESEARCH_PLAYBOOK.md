Institutional Research Playbook (IRP)

Operational Manual for Evidence-Based Crypto Research

Version: v1.0

⸻

Purpose

This document defines the practical operating procedures used to conduct institutional-grade crypto research.

Unlike the Research Standard, which defines research principles, and the Research Framework, which defines analytical thinking, the Playbook defines execution.

It answers a single question:

What should a researcher do next?

Every research task should follow this playbook.

⸻

Research Operating Principle

Professional researchers do not begin with conclusions.

Professional researchers begin with observations.

Every investigation starts with measurable reality.

⸻

Standard Research Workflow

Every research task follows this sequence.

Observe
↓
Classify
↓
Investigate
↓
Validate
↓
Challenge
↓
Synthesize
↓
Communicate
↓
Monitor
↓
Revise

Skipping steps requires explicit justification.

⸻

Phase 1 — Observe

Objective

Capture observable facts without interpretation.

Questions

* What happened?
* When did it happen?
* Where did it happen?
* Which protocol or asset is involved?
* Which data confirms the event?

Output

Observation Log

⸻

Phase 2 — Classify

Determine the type of event.

Examples

Protocol Event

Market Event

Governance Event

Onchain Activity

Security Incident

Regulatory Event

Macro Event

Classification determines the investigation path.

⸻

Phase 3 — Investigate

Collect supporting evidence.

Evidence Categories

Protocol

* Official documentation
* Upgrade proposals
* Governance discussions

Onchain

* Transactions
* Wallet activity
* Exchange flows
* Token movements
* Smart contract interactions

Market

* Funding Rates
* Open Interest
* Spot Volume
* Derivatives

Macro

* Interest rates
* ETF flows
* Regulatory announcements

Never rely upon a single category.

⸻

Phase 4 — Validate

Every dataset must pass validation.

Validation Checklist

□ Authentic

□ Current

□ Complete

□ Independently Verifiable

□ Internally Consistent

Datasets failing validation must not support conclusions.

⸻

Phase 5 — Challenge

Actively search for competing explanations.

Questions

What evidence weakens this explanation?

Which assumptions remain unverified?

Could another hypothesis explain the same observation?

Professional researchers attempt to falsify their own conclusions.

⸻

Phase 6 — Synthesize

Combine evidence into structured hypotheses.

Each hypothesis should contain

Observation

↓

Evidence

↓

Counter Evidence

↓

Assumptions

↓

Confidence

↓

Evidence Confidence Level (ECL)

Avoid binary conclusions.

Rank competing hypotheses instead.

⸻

Phase 7 — Communicate

Research reports should communicate:

Executive Summary

One-page overview.

⸻

Key Findings

Evidence-supported discoveries.

⸻

Supporting Evidence

Primary evidence only.

⸻

Risks

Known uncertainties.

⸻

Alternative Explanations

Competing hypotheses.

⸻

Monitoring Items

Future developments requiring observation.

⸻

Phase 8 — Monitor

Research continues after publication.

Monitor

* Protocol upgrades
* Governance votes
* Exchange activity
* ETF flows
* Stablecoin issuance
* Macro changes
* Regulatory developments

Research without monitoring becomes obsolete.

⸻

Phase 9 — Revise

Research must evolve.

Revision triggers include

* Better evidence
* Better datasets
* Protocol changes
* Historical corrections
* Invalidated assumptions

Old conclusions should never be protected from revision.

⸻

Standard Research Checklist

Before publishing

□ Observation recorded

□ Research question defined

□ Primary sources reviewed

□ Independent evidence collected

□ Counter evidence documented

□ Alternative hypotheses evaluated

□ Evidence Confidence Level assigned

□ Assumptions documented

□ Unknowns acknowledged

□ References completed

Institutional Research Questions

Every investigation should answer:

What happened?

Why did it happen?

Who participated?

Who benefited?

Who was affected?

What assumptions am I making?

Which evidence supports my interpretation?

Which evidence weakens it?

What remains unknown?

What should I monitor next?

⸻

Institutional Mindset

Do not seek confirmation.

Seek understanding.

Do not defend hypotheses.

Test hypotheses.

Do not predict certainty.

Estimate probabilities.

⸻

Common Research Failures

Avoid

* Starting with conclusions
* Selecting evidence selectively
* Ignoring contradictory evidence
* Confusing correlation with causation
* Overconfidence
* Outdated datasets
* Missing historical context

⸻

Definition of Completion

A research task is complete only when:

* Evidence is documented.
* Competing explanations are evaluated.
* Unknowns are acknowledged.
* Confidence matches evidence.
* Future monitoring is defined.

Research without monitoring is incomplete.

⸻

Final Principle

Institutional researchers are not rewarded for being certain.

They are rewarded for making better decisions under uncertainty.

This playbook exists to standardize that process.
