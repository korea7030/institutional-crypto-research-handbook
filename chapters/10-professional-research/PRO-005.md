## 1. Learning Objectives

- Explain why traceability matters.
- Build claim-to-source linkage systems.
- Preserve reproducibility under revision.

## 2. Executive Summary

`RESEARCH_STANDARD.md` states that every important conclusion must be traceable back to its supporting evidence.[^ref-rs]

`AI_AUTHORING_GUIDE.md` strengthens this requirement by demanding claim extraction, cross-verification, evidence labels, and independently verifiable references.[^ref-aiag]

Evidence tracking is therefore not clerical overhead. It is what makes research auditable.

## 3. Minimum Tracking Standard

For each material claim, record:

- claim text or identifier,
- source location,
- source type,
- access date or version,
- confidence label,
- reviewer note if contested.

## 4. Common Failure Modes

- citation without actual claim mapping,
- source lists that cannot answer which evidence supports which conclusion,
- silent source substitution during revisions.

## 5. Institutional Thinking

- Traceability turns research from persuasive prose into inspectable work.
- Weak evidence tracking usually surfaces later as overconfidence and revision friction.
