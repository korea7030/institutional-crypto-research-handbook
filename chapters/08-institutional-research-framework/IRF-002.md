## 1. Learning Objectives

- Derive questions from observations.
- Distinguish open research questions from loaded framing.
- Generate competing investigation paths.

## 2. Executive Summary

`RESEARCH_STANDARD.md` states that every observation should generate one or more research questions and that research quality depends on question quality.[^ref-rs]

`RESEARCH_FRAMEWORK.md` makes the same point directly: poor questions produce poor research.[^ref-rf]

Good questions widen the evidence search. Bad questions narrow it too early and often embed the answer they want.

## 3. Good Question Design

Useful questions often ask:

- why now,
- who or what is driving the change,
- what alternative explanations fit,
- how unusual the event is historically,
- what evidence would falsify the preferred explanation.

## 4. Bad Question Design

Weak questions:

- assume motive without proof,
- ask only bullish or only bearish versions,
- collapse multiple unknowns into one loaded sentence.

## 5. Institutional Thinking

- Questions are the bridge between raw data and structured inquiry.
- Better questions produce broader evidence and better falsification opportunities.
