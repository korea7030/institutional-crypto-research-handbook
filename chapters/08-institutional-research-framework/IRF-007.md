## 1. Learning Objectives

- Assign probability to competing explanations.
- Distinguish confidence from certainty.
- Update probabilities when evidence changes.

## 2. Executive Summary

`RESEARCH_STANDARD.md` states that conclusions are the best-supported explanations given currently available evidence, not permanent truths.[^ref-rs]

`RESEARCH_FRAMEWORK.md` explicitly instructs researchers to estimate probability, never certainty, and gives example percentage rankings across hypotheses.[^ref-rf]

The aim is calibration, not theatrical precision.

## 3. Practical Standard

Probability assessment should reflect:

- evidence quality,
- evidence breadth,
- unresolved assumptions,
- strength of counter evidence,
- structural uncertainty.

## 4. Common Errors

- assigning false precision,
- mapping probability to conviction size without risk adjustment,
- refusing to update when new evidence appears.

## 5. Institutional Thinking

- Better probability work comes from disciplined updates, not bigger decimal points.
- A modest but honest probability estimate is more useful than a confident unsupported claim.
