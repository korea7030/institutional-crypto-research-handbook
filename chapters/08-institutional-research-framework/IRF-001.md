## 1. Learning Objectives

- Define observation as measurable reality.
- Separate observation from explanation.
- Build cleaner observation logs.

## 2. Executive Summary

`RESEARCH_STANDARD.md` states that observations describe measurable reality and must not contain interpretation.[^ref-rs]

`RESEARCH_FRAMEWORK.md` repeats that research begins with measurable facts and that observations must contain no interpretation.[^ref-rf]

`RESEARCH_PLAYBOOK.md` operationalizes this by instructing researchers to capture observable facts first and only then classify and investigate them.[^ref-rp]

The institutional point is simple: weak observation contaminates every downstream step. If the first sentence already embeds a theory, the research process is no longer testing reality. It is defending a narrative.

## 3. Core Standard

Good observation answers:

- what happened,
- when,
- where,
- on which protocol, asset, or metric basis.

Bad observation sneaks in:

- motive,
- causality,
- confidence language unsupported by evidence.

## 4. Practical Examples

Observation:

- BTC exchange reserve decreased over the week.

Not observation:

- Smart money is accumulating BTC before a breakout.

The second sentence contains hypothesis, motive, and market implication, none of which are yet established by the observed data alone.

## 5. Institutional Thinking

- Observation quality determines research quality.
- A clean observation log reduces narrative drift.
- Researchers should prefer dull factual clarity over premature explanatory confidence.
