## 1. Observation

As AI narratives expanded globally, crypto markets began repricing projects that claimed to coordinate compute, intelligence, or agent networks onchain.

## 2. Source Context

Bittensor's whitepaper describes a peer-to-peer intelligence market in which participants are rewarded for producing informational value.[^ref-bittensor-wp]

Bittensor's documentation migration notice also shows the ecosystem maturing into a maintained developer and operational stack rather than remaining only a thesis document.[^ref-bittensor-docs]

## 3. Event Structure

AI × Crypto narratives gained traction where investors believed blockchains could coordinate:

- machine intelligence markets,
- incentives for model contribution,
- decentralized access to AI resources,
- tokenized reward structures around AI participation.

## 4. Why It Mattered

This narrative tested whether crypto could attach itself to a dominant external technology wave in a way that was economically native rather than merely symbolic.

## 5. Lessons

- Narrative adjacency alone is weak.
- Stronger cases require a clear answer to: why does this need a token, a chain, or an onchain market?
- Thematic spillover can drive price faster than underlying utility develops.

## 6. Institutional Thinking

- AI × Crypto should be studied as a filtration problem: which projects actually use crypto for coordination, payments, or incentive alignment, and which merely borrow AI attention.
- The quality bar should be higher than "mentions AI."
