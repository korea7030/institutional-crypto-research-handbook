## 1. Observation

By 2026, Ethereum was no longer only one execution environment. Ethereum.org explicitly described Ethereum as a network with hundreds of blockchains built on top of it.[^ref-eth-l2]

## 2. Source Context

Ethereum.org's layer 2 documentation states that Ethereum's strength and security provide a platform for other networks to build upon and that Ethereum has become more cost-effective, faster, and accessible through these networks.[^ref-eth-l2]

Ethereum.org's scaling roadmap states that Ethereum is scaled using layer 2s, also known as rollups.[^ref-eth-scaling]

Arbitrum and Optimism documentation show the implementation side of this shift through dedicated rollup platforms and standard bridge systems.[^ref-arb][^ref-op]

## 3. Event Structure

Layer 2 expansion was not just more chains appearing. It was a strategic redefinition of Ethereum's scaling model:

- execution off mainnet,
- settlement and data anchoring on Ethereum,
- asset movement through bridges,
- application migration toward cheaper environments.

## 4. Why It Mattered

This changed how analysts measured Ethereum growth:

- not only by L1 fees or L1 addresses,
- but by total ecosystem usage across rollups and bridge-linked environments.

## 5. Lessons

- Scaling success can fragment surface metrics while strengthening the base platform.
- L2 growth improves user economics but adds bridge, fragmentation, and governance complexity.
- Ethereum's post-2022 thesis increasingly became "network of networks."

## 6. Institutional Thinking

- Researchers should avoid treating L2 proliferation as dilution by default.
- The right question is whether L2 growth strengthens Ethereum settlement demand and ecosystem stickiness.
