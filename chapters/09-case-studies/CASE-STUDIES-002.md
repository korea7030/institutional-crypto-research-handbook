## 1. Observation

In March 2020, crypto sold off in the same broad liquidity event that hit global risk assets during the early COVID-19 shock.

## 2. Key Dates

- March 15, 2020: the Federal Reserve cut rates to near zero and announced actions to support the flow of credit to households and businesses.[^ref-fed-0315]
- March 23, 2020: the Federal Reserve announced extensive new measures to support the economy.[^ref-fed-0323]

## 3. Event Structure

The core analytical point is that the event was first a liquidity crisis, not a crypto-idiosyncratic thesis revision.

In liquidity panics, investors sell what they can, not only what they dislike. Crypto's drawdown therefore functioned as a stress test of:

- market depth,
- leverage fragility,
- and correlation behavior in forced-risk-off conditions.

## 4. Why It Mattered

March 2020 showed that:

- crypto was not insulated from global dollar stress,
- macro liquidity could dominate local narratives,
- and recovery potential depended partly on broader policy stabilization.

## 5. Lessons

- In crisis conditions, correlation structures can compress violently.
- "Digital gold" narratives are weakest during acute forced deleveraging.
- Policy response timing matters for all risk assets, including crypto.

## 6. Institutional Thinking

- The correct question is not whether crypto fell in March 2020. It is why it fell together with everything else.
- Researchers should separate long-run monetary narratives from short-run liquidity mechanics.
